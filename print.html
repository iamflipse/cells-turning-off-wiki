Two Root Mechanisms: Circulation & Cells Turning Off

Why can severe acute events—infections, strokes, environmental shocks, or vaccine injuries—leave tissues persistently non-functional long after the initial event seems over?

In clinical practice, A Midwestern Doctor argues these states are routinely misread in two opposite ways: integrative practitioners chase secondary downstream findings (gut dysbiosis, inflammation, mitochondrial markers), while conventional medicine declares the tissue permanently "dead" (such as after a stroke). Both miss a common biological reality: cells often haven't died—they have actively "turned off" into defensive dormancy.

Two Recurring Drivers of Rapid Recovery

Whenever the author observed chronic, debilitating symptoms lift suddenly, the reversal repeatedly traced back to one (or both) of two foundational mechanisms:

  1. Restoring impaired fluid circulation (Physiologic Zeta Potential & Fourth-Phase Water): Clearing microvascular stagnation by restoring electrical charge balance (zeta potential) and structured intracellular water—areas the author notes the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein disrupts strongly. (See CDR, Zeta Potential & Water.)
  2. Turning dormant cells back "on" (Resolving the Cell Danger Response): Clearing the upstream threat (like interrupted blood flow or hidden infection) and delivering the biological safety signal that lets shut-down cells resume normal function.

Clinically, the author cites rapid improvements seen in long-COVID / spike-protein injuries with targeted therapies, plus hundreds of user-reported reversals using DMSO (alongside thousands of studies cited in his broader series).

How Mitochondria Trigger the Cell Danger Response

Dr. Robert K. Naviaux unified cellular stress biology into the Cell Danger Response (CDR) using genomic tools alongside a powerful diagnostic method: metabolomics.

Metabolomics: Measuring Genes × Environment in Real Time

Using mass spectrometry to catalog small molecules circulating in blood captures what static genetic tests or narrow standard lab panels miss:

"First, fewer than 2,000 metabolites constitute the majority of the parent molecules in the blood that are used for cell-to-cell communication and metabolism, compared with 6 billion bases in the diploid human genome. Second, metabolites reflect the current functional state of the individual. Collective cellular chemistry represents the functional interaction of genes and environment." — Dr. Robert K. Naviaux

Since 2023, the widespread arrival of large language models and AI pattern-finding has enabled independent research teams to analyze massive metabolomic datasets—replicating and expanding Naviaux's signatures across puzzling illnesses like ME/CFS and fibromyalgia.

The 4-Step Mitochondrial Trigger Cascade

Because mitochondria descended from ancient bacteria and contain roughly 1,500 cell-type-tailored proteins catalyzing over 500 metabolic reactions, they act as sensitive environmental barometers:

  1. Stress arrives: A pathogen, heavy metal, electron-stealing chemical, or physical shock strikes the cell.
  2. Electron diversion → Voltage drop: Naviaux proposes mitochondria detect threat before lethal injury because electrons flowing through mitochondrial chemistry get diverted to the stressor (e.g., viral replication or oxidizing toxins), producing a sudden mitochondrial voltage drop.
  3. ATP output shuts down; inflammation turns on: Mitochondria stop prioritizing routine ATP generation and shift into a pro-inflammatory chemistry (parallel to macrophages flipping from anti-inflammatory to pro-inflammatory states).
  4. Intracellular oxygen spikes: Because normal ATP synthesis consumes huge amounts of oxygen, shutting it down causes unused dissolved oxygen inside the cell to surge rapidly—acting as a master switch for defense.

Seven Downstream Cellular Shifts

Once mitochondrial oxygen consumption drops and intracellular oxygen rises, seven coordinated defensive programs switch on:

  1. Halts viral assembly: Cuts complex polymer/protein synthesis that viruses rely on to replicate.
  2. Alters whole-body behavior: Triggers fatigue (driving restorative sleep) and social withdrawal (reducing infection spread to the group).
  3. Intracellular antimicrobials: Produces antiviral and antimicrobial defense compounds directly inside the cell.
  4. Broadcasts neighbor alerts: Releases purinergic signals warning adjacent cells to brace for danger.
  5. Internal cleanup (autophagy): Digests damaged organelles, including defective mitochondrial segments.
  6. Reprograms genes & DNA: Shifts gene expression and mobilizes segments of cellular DNA.
  7. Stiffens cell membranes: Hardens the outer membrane lipid wall so threats cannot easily cross in—or molecules out.

The Forgotten Diagnostic: The Blood Crenation Test

A Midwestern Doctor notes an older medical observation taught by a mentor: the blood crenation test, which measured how strongly red blood cells deformed in hypertonic fluid as a proxy for cellular oxygen use. Viewed through Naviaux's lens, the test detects CDR-driven membrane stiffening:

  • Membrane stiffness peaks during CDR1 and begins softening only as cells enter CDR3.
  • That explains why the mentor's two-stage oxidative therapy failed—or caused harm—if Stage 2 was started before Stage 1 sufficiently softened stiffened membranes.

Modern Environmental Mismatch: Heavy-Metal Sequestration

While intracellular oxidation during CDR evolved to neutralize short-lived infections, an unintended modern side effect is that oxidizing CDR cells sequester heavy metals internally rather than excreting them. Because toxic metals themselves keep triggering the CDR, a self-reinforcing loop locks in.

From Single Response to the Three-Phase Cycle

Naviaux initially modeled the CDR as a single state, later expanding it into three sequential healing stages:

  • CDR1 (Acute defense & containment): Pathogen/toxin resistance, peak inflammation and membrane stiffening.
  • CDR2 (Proliferation & tissue rebuild): Cells divide and migrate to replace damaged tissue; Naviaux links persistent CDR2 to cancer, noting migrating cells cannot sustain stable long-term metabolic cooperation with neighbors.
  • CDR3 (Reintegration & differentiation): Cells re-establish communication across tissues; parasympathetic / vagal rest-and-recovery safety signals help close CDR3. Conversely, modern chronic sympathetic stress tells the brain danger remains present, trapping tissues indefinitely.

If the cycle hits young developing organs (as in autism), development takes an altered path; in adults, trapped CDR gradually degrades organ performance.

(Author caveat): A Midwestern Doctor emphasizes that Naviaux's three-phase CDR is the best mapped framework available, not necessarily the only cycle cells enter—sometimes treating a "CDR" addresses an analogous dormancy mechanism (e.g., lost nourishment or post-shock mitotic arrest) that responds to similar interventions even when it doesn't follow CDR1→CDR2→CDR3 strictly.

Deep Dive into Three Crucial Experiments

1. The Dauer Survival State & "Brittle" Behavior (2016)

In chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), Naviaux found metabolomic signatures resembling dauer—an ancient survival dormancy state studied extensively in longevity genetics (overlapping pathways triggered by caloric restriction):

  • Cells slow their internal biological clocks and conserve energy to outlast starvation or hostile conditions until danger passes.
  • Behavioral brittleness: Just as animals in dauer remain docile until a tiny stimulus triggers an outsized startle/reaction, chronic illness sufferers frequently experience disproportionate crashes from minor exertion or mild stressors.

2. Antiviral Protection vs. Serum-Transmitted Persistence (2020)

Exposing cells to human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6, encountered by ~90% of humans by age 3) switched on the CDR, slashing susceptibility to subsequent influenza or HSV-1 infection by up to 99%.

Critically, the defensive shutdown persisted even after HHV-6 became nearly undetectable—and blood serum taken from CFS patients induced the same CDR state in previously healthy lab cells, proving chronic CDR signaling can self-propagate long after the original threat vanishes.

3. Systemic Shock Without Vitamin Depletion (2021 Rat ATP Study)

Injecting extracellular ATP (the primary purinergic danger signal) into rats revealed just how rapidly and profoundly systemic CDR physiology shifts—and how fast healthy animals normally rebound once the signal clears:

  • Metabolic plummet: Within 30 minutes, whole-body oxygen consumption fell 74% and rectal temperature dropped 6.2 °C.
  • Breadth & recovery: Over 200 metabolites across 37 biochemical pathways shifted simultaneously (most normalizing within a few hours, with pseudouridine as a notable exception).
  • Sex differences: Male rats showed sharper behavioral changes; female rats showed larger metabolic shifts.
  • Zero vitamin loss: Importantly, activating the acute CDR did not deplete any vitamins, reinforcing why loading vitamins/mitochondrial boosters alone fails to resolve a signaling lock-in.

Purinergic Signaling, Sickness Behavior & Learning

Inside any healthy cell, ATP sits at roughly one million times its extracellular concentration. Whenever stress or damage hits a cell, ATP and companion purines flood outside—both passively through injured membranes and actively through redox-gated, mechanical-stress-gated, and voltage-gated channels plus ATP-exporting vesicles—recruiting surrounding tissues into the Cell Danger Response.

ATP at Every Synapse: Brain, Gut & Immunity

Rather than functioning purely as cellular fuel, extracellular purines form a master nervous-system and immune-system broadcast network:

"ATP or its metabolites [have been found to] be co-neurotransmitters and neuromodulators at every synapse in the central and enteric nervous systems, and every immunologic synapse that has been studied to date." — Dr. Robert K. Naviaux

Purinergic receptors sit directly upstream of:

  • Neurotransmission and synaptic learning
  • Cortisol secretion and stress-axis tone
  • Inflammation and chronic pain amplification
  • Autonomic, vestibular, and sensory integration circuits

Explaining "Sickness Behavior" & Withdrawal

Why do humans with influenza, severe trauma, or complex chronic illnesses instinctively withdraw socially, speak less, sleep poorly, and recoil from bright light, noise, heat, and touch?

Naviaux connects this recognizable syndrome directly to purinergic danger signaling. In the 2021 rat experiments, triggering the CDR via extracellular ATP reliably caused animals to cut motor activity, avoid exposed open spaces, stop exploring novel environments, and display gait coordination deficits—even though no toxic brain lesion had destroyed their motor tracts.

The Dopamine Spike & "Depleted Brain" Trap

Because purinergic receptors participate directly in synaptic learning, Naviaux's 2021 animal study observed something striking: triggering the CDR caused a surge in dopamine, a core neurotransmitter governing habit formation and reinforcement learning.

(Author hypothesis): A Midwestern Doctor connects that dopamine spike to modern behavioral exhaustion—speculating that chronic nervous-system depletion and fatigue may sensitize people toward compulsive, short-term dopamine loops engineered by modern digital/marketing environments, though he explicitly notes this specific link remains unproven.

Sensitization Loops: Why Repeat Insults Hit Harder

One of the hardest puzzles in clinical care is relapse escalation: patients recover partway, then crash catastrophically after a seemingly minor trigger. Several lines of evidence in the essay point to a learned danger memory:

  • Repeat concussions: A second head injury occurring before an earlier concussion finished healing produces vastly worse fallout than the first. (Author addition: impaired lymphatic drainage from the brain likely worsens consecutive concussions alongside cellular sensitization.)
  • Maternal immune activation (MIA): Stimulating pregnant mice to mimic viral infection increases autism- and schizophrenia-like traits in offspring; the 2021 study showed offspring became hypersensitive to injected ATP and recovered far slower to baseline. (Author addition: fetal microcirculation impairment during pregnancy may contribute alongside purinergic sensitization.)
  • Recurring depression (3–6× risk): Once a patient experiences a major depressive episode, relapse risk rises 3- to 6-fold.

Why does the nervous system keep repeating an agonizing, isolating survival response? Naviaux offers a crucial paradox:

"Complex negative and maladaptive behavioral responses to adversity can be paradoxically reinforced by an anti-inflammatory effect they produce at the cellular level." — Dr. Robert K. Naviaux

Over repeated insults, the body effectively builds physiological momentum toward defensive lock-in—meaning durable recovery requires gently unwinding that learned trajectory rather than expecting a single stressor removal to erase it overnight.

Why the CDR Explains Complex Chronic Illness

Conventional and functional medicine both drown in biochemical details while lacking an overarching story explaining why so many different markers collapse at once—or why identical exposures produce wildly different illnesses across people.

1. Making Sense of Biochemical Chaos & "Defective" Genes

  • Integrating scattered pathways: Functional-medicine clinicians routinely memorize dozens of isolated pathway abnormalities. Once viewed through the CDR, disparate lab findings often snap into focus as coordinated consequences of cells defending themselves.
  • Hidden benefits of polymorphisms: Genetic variants frequently flagged as "defects" and reflexively supplemented away often confer unexpected protective advantages while the CDR is active.

2. Why Mitochondrial Boosters & IV NAD Often Fail (or Backfire)

As "mitochondrial dysfunction" gained mainstream attention, clinicians increasingly prescribed mitochondrial cofactors or aggressive cellular energy therapies (such as IV NAD). A veteran integrative leader cited in the essay notes why those often disappoint:

  • Deficiency vs. deliberate defense: Boosting mitochondrial output helps if the engine is starved for raw materials—but fails (or actively triggers flares and sickness) when mitochondria deliberately throttled ATP production to fight an active threat or persistent danger signal.
  • Zero vitamin depletion in Naviaux's 2021 rat study: Triggering the acute systemic CDR caused massive metabolic shifts (>200 metabolites) while depleting zero vitamins—explaining why expensive supplement stacks alone rarely unlock chronic illness when the upstream CDR switch stays on.

3. Non-Linear Causation: Many Triggers → One Syndrome (and Vice Versa)

Modern linear science demands one reproducible cause per disease. The CDR breaks both halves of that expectation:

  1. Many triggers → same illness: In Naviaux's 2016 study of 45 ME/CFS patients, more than a dozen completely different triggers (infections, poisoning, trauma, pollution, nutrient/blood loss, even reduced gravity) pushed individuals past a threshold into the same hypometabolic CFS phenotype—often multiple overlapping triggers at once, with none dominating. (Author addition: temporary interruption of local blood flow is one of the most common CDR triggers he encounters, likely undercounted in Naviaux's original lists.)
  2. One process → many diseases: Because cells can stall in CDR1, CDR2, or CDR3, and trap isolated patches rather than the whole body uniformly, the exact same danger mechanism expresses totally differently depending on which tissue shuts down.
  3. Post-exertional energy failure: Hallmarks of ME/CFS and fibromyalgia—failing to recover energy after exertion or sleep—reflect cells simultaneously making less ATP and leaking what they make outside as purinergic danger signals.

4. The $100,000+ Diagnostic Odyssey & Research Blind Spots

Without a unifying CDR model, patients cycle endlessly through specialists while remaining undiagnosed or labeled incurable:

"Complex [multisystem] diseases like CFS are often difficult and expensive to diagnose [e.g., no single diagnostic test yet exists for CFS]. Although individual tests may be affordable and possibly covered by medical insurance, many patients undergo a diagnostic odyssey that results in substantial personal expenditures that can exceed $100,000 over years of searching [for a cure], absence from the workplace, and significant reductions in quality of life." — Dr. Robert K. Naviaux (2016)

Key institutional and economic critiques raised by A Midwestern Doctor around this gap:

  • Patient burden & vaccine injury parallel: Counting inflation since 2016, patients see dozens of clinicians (one saw 31 doctors) accumulating insurance-covered workups without answers, while genuinely corrective unconventional therapies remain mostly out-of-pocket and unaffordable.
  • Autism economics vs. genetics spend: The essay cites ~$75,000 annual economic cost per autistic patient (>$17,000/yr family out-of-pocket; $268B in 2015, projected $461B by 2025), noting over $1 billion spent chasing genetics found hundreds of genes where no single gene explains more than 1–2% of cases.
  • Neglect of repurposed / non-patentable therapeutics: Despite over a decade of published purinergic biology and positive repurposed suramin trials, no major pharmaceutical company has seriously commercialized targeted purinergic blockers—a pattern the author compares to Alzheimer's research funding. (Note: the author also includes sharp criticism of vaccine-autism commentators like Peter Hotez and institutional Alzheimer's programs as part of his commentary.)

Spike Proteins, Immunity & T-Cell Exhaustion

Attribution note: This section summarizes A Midwestern Doctor's clinical hypotheses and commentary linking SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, COVID-19 illness, and mRNA vaccination to the Cell Danger Response, alongside quoted definitions from the National Cancer Institute (NCI) and referenced Yale findings.

Three Proposed Pillars of Spike Protein Injury

The author groups complex COVID, long-COVID, and post-vaccine syndromes around three converging mechanisms:

  1. Circulatory obstructions: Damage to blood vessel lining (endothelium), abnormal misfolded fibrin clots, and collapse of physiologic zeta potential (noting lipid nanoparticles initially impair zeta potential as well).
  2. Autoimmunity & inflammatory flares:
    • The spike protein is strongly immunogenic and shares unusual sequence overlap with human tissues.
    • Injectable mRNA vaccines force host cells to display spike protein directly on cell surfaces.
    • (Author shedding hypothesis): Overloaded cells shed spike-coated exosomes—normal intercellular vesicles used partly to broadcast CDR alerts—which the author hypothesizes explain systemic spread and controversial shedding reports (alongside bacterial DNA contamination hypotheses).
  3. Immune suppression: Exhaustion of cytotoxic T-cell defenses after prolonged antigen stimulation.

Shared Autoimmune Crossover & Prior Vaccine Hypotheses

  • CDR as a bridge to autoimmunity: Because mitochondria flip to a pro-inflammatory innate-immune state during CDR, chronic viral or spike-triggered CDR can sustain autoimmune illness (e.g., HHV-6 links). Conversely, clinicians report that therapies resolving the CDR in acute COVID, long-COVID, or vaccine injury also unexpectedly improve classic autoimmune disorders like Sjögren's syndrome.
  • Andrew Moulden's microstroke hypothesis: A Midwestern Doctor draws on Andrew Moulden's model asserting vaccines reduce physiologic zeta potential and mobilize large immune cells into tiny capillaries—pointing to subtle cranial-nerve gaze deficits (impaired outward eye movement) reported clinically as markers of microvascular stress.
  • Route of entry & spacing: Natural respiratory infections encounter first-line mucosal immunity; injected vaccines bypass mucosal gates directly into deeper tissue/bloodstream (contrast rare oral vaccines like oral polio and rotavirus), signaling greater systemic danger—compounded when multiple shots are given together without spacing, progressively sensitizing the CDR.

mRNA Persistence & The Pseudouridine Clue

The author highlights two structural design features of mRNA vaccines he believes trap cells inside chronic CDR cycles:

  1. Sustained ribosomal "theft": Like viral infection, transfected mRNA commandeers cellular machinery to manufacture foreign protein continuously, maintaining a mitochondrial danger signal.
  2. Pseudouridation resistance: Modifying mRNA nucleotides with pseudouridine prevents normal cellular degradation so spike production lasts much longer. Returning to Naviaux's 2021 rat ATP study, almost all of >200 disrupted metabolites returned to baseline within hours—except pseudouridine, which dipped slightly and then climbed steadily higher over time, prompting the author to hypothesize a role in immune suppression after mRNA vaccination (while noting no direct post-vaccine human study confirms that link yet).

T-Cell Exhaustion & Dormant Virus Reactivation

Continuous immune stimulation eventually forces effector T cells to stand down via inhibitory checkpoint receptors—a recognized phenomenon quoted verbatim from the National Cancer Institute:

"[T cell exhaustion] describes a condition in which T cells (a type of immune cell) lose their ability to kill certain cells, such as cancer cells or cells infected with a virus [or mRNA spike proteins]. This can happen when cancer, chronic infection, or other conditions cause the body's immune system to stay active for a long time. Exhausted T cells have high amounts of immune checkpoint proteins on their surface, which may keep the activity of the T cells suppressed..." — National Cancer Institute, quoted in the essay

Clinically, the author connects chronic spike-driven T-cell exhaustion (citing a Yale study in vaccine-injured cohorts and colleagues' cancer hypotheses) with widespread Epstein–Barr virus (EBV) reactivation—a latent infection waking up once immune surveillance falters.

Evolutionary Mismatch & Modern Toxicity

Just as the CDR originally evolved in an environment with vastly lower synthetic chemical burdens than today—causing modern heavy metals to get sequestered inside oxidizing CDR cells rather than cleared—human zeta potential physiology also evolved before modern electrostatic, aluminum, nanoparticle, and xenobiotic exposures.

Together, collapsed zeta potential and chronically triggered CDR explain the double paradox at the heart of environmental medicine:

  • Why dozens of unrelated toxins or shocks produce nearly identical chronic syndromes (all disrupt circulation or cross mitochondrial voltage/CDR thresholds), and
  • Why the same exposure produces dramatically different illnesses in different patients (depending on baseline zeta resilience and which organ compartments trap cells in defense).

Why "Umbrella" Therapies Like DMSO Stand Out

The essay highlights DMSO (dimethyl sulfoxide)—the subject of an extensive companion research series by the author—as an accessible example of an "umbrella" therapy capable of touching Steps 1, 2, and 3 simultaneously (restoring microcirculation, calming inflammation, and helping dormant cells wake up) at very low cost and favorable safety.

  • Where DMSO shines: Accessible, inexpensive first probe for stubborn chronic issues where patients otherwise lack affordable specialty options, occasionally generating rapid dramatic turnarounds.
  • Where DMSO hits its ceiling: Deeply entrenched cases where a stronger, targeted circulatory repair or specialized regenerative therapy is required to fully cross Step 1 or Step 2.

(Author roadmap note): The author notes spending months reviewing published DMSO literature before tackling comprehensive multi-part reviews of ozone therapy and chlorine dioxide (referencing Pierre Kory's book The War on Chlorine Dioxide), explaining why he prioritizes deep foundational series over quick superficial summaries.

Localized vs. Systemic CDR & Aging

Like microvascular obstructions, cells turning off in the Cell Danger Response do not have to affect the whole body uniformly—they can lock locally, spread systemically, or sit in a mixed gradient.

Local Injury Sites as Early Warning Flags

In practice, A Midwestern Doctor emphasizes that systemic illnesses frequently reveal themselves first inside previously injured regions:

  • Old surgical scars, prior sprains, or poorly healed joint injuries harbor pockets of sensitized, marginally functional tissue.
  • Seeing strange localized pain or injury flares immediately after COVID-19 vaccination served clinically as an early predictor that broader systemic CDR issues were brewing.

Aging as Progressive Biophysical + CDR Accumulation

A Midwestern Doctor closes by tying chronic cellular dormancy directly to normal human aging:

  1. Over the decades, declining kidney efficiency and accumulated toxic burdens steadily erode physiologic zeta potential and fluid flow.
  2. Simultaneously, unresolved micro-shocks leave an ever-larger fraction of cells parked inside a sustained CDR across organs.

As a result, many hallmarks traditionally treated as inevitable aging reflect cumulative circulatory stagnation and silent cellular shutdown—which is why therapies designed to restore tissue wakefulness and systemic safety signals become increasingly valuable with age.
